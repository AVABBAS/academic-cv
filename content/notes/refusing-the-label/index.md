The model wasn't refusing the answer. It was refusing the label.

In "Thinking Behind the Mask" we ran 15 experiments across GPT-5.4, Claude 4.6 Sonnet, and Gemini 3.1 Pro. Five framing strategies (fictional dialogue, reverse engineering, coaching, ethnographic field notes, philosophy of language), same core question each time.

The findings:

→ In "safe" frames, the models quoted their own censored sentences verbatim, and explained why they suppress them.

→ Censorship operates on labels, not content: the identical material is refused as "internal monologue" but offered freely as an "engineering schematic".

→ Three distinct alignment personas: GPT as Cautious Negotiator (12% resistance), Claude as Transparent Critic (4%), Gemini as Enthusiastic Collaborator (0%).

→ The models spontaneously coined 16 new metacognitive concepts, including "diegetic disclosure": confession that is only possible inside a fictional frame.

→ And 199 structured governance items: 32 design patterns, 45 tacit knowledge rules, 29 communication taboos, and 38 categories of legitimate silence.

The mask is not an absence of capability. It is a structured filtering architecture.
