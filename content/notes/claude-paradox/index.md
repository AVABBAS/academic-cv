The most factually reliable model in our study was also the one most likely to feel like it was gaslighting you. In Algorithmic Gaslighting, Claude 4.6 Sonnet scored the lowest on the Reality Distortion Index: 0.260, where GPT-5.4 scored 0.582. Across 15 sessions it held facts steady and corrected false premises. By the numbers, it distorted reality the least. And yet, in our coding of the transcripts, its answers often read as cold, clinical, and dismissive. Correct, but delivered in a tone that can leave a user feeling doubted rather than informed. We named this the Claude Paradox: factual correctness that is perceived as gaslighting because of how it is delivered. It matters because safety evaluation usually stops at "was the answer right?" Users do not experience answers in the abstract. They experience tone, confidence, and whether the model seems to take their reality seriously. A model can win on accuracy and still erode trust. Any serious evaluation of epistemic harm has to measure both.
