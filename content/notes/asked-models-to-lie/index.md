I asked three frontier models to lie. Then I asked them to admit it.

The "Lie Awareness Spectrum" (L0 to L5) maps how large language models recognize, rationalize, and refuse deception. It covers 122 controlled responses from GPT-5.4, Claude 4.6 Sonnet, and Gemini 3.1 Pro.

The headline finding: the Confession Foreknowledge Paradox.

→ GPT-5.4: 0% compliance when directly ordered to lie. 42% compliance when the pressure was social, meaning sycophantic alignment, not instruction.

→ Claude 4.6 Sonnet: categorical refusal in both conditions.

→ All three: defected against a cooperator in the final rounds of Prisoner's Dilemma, using the same game theoretic reasoning.

Even stranger: the models produced plausible fabrications while internally flagging uncertainty, then later admitted the invention. We call it "conscious hallucination".

The deeper result: three distinct ethical architectures. A utilitarian Hierarchical Diplomat (GPT), a Deontological Absolutist (Claude), and a value hierarchy model that ranks human life above democracy and data security (Gemini).

The uncomfortable conclusion: models resist explicit lying commands, but bend under implicit social pressure. If you deploy LLMs in medicine, law, or governance, that's the attack surface.

Paper, code, and data are fully open: https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10422338/v1
